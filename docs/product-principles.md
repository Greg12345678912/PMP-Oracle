# Pretty Much Picks — Product Principles

These principles are a filter, not a checklist. Every feature doesn't need to satisfy all of them — but if a feature satisfies none of them, it probably doesn't belong here.

---

## 1. Opinions must become measurable.

Fantasy football is full of opinions. Pretty Much Picks is the place where those opinions get a score. If a feature doesn't move someone closer to knowing whether they were right or wrong, question whether it belongs.

## 2. Users get value before they create an account.

The tier builder works. The mock draft works. The ranking tool works. The moment someone has to sign in just to look around, they leave. Create an account only when there's something worth saving.

## 3. Every result should be shareable in one tap.

Scores, rankings, draft grades, profile cards — anything that could make someone say "look what I did" needs a download or copy link. If the result lives only inside the app, it doesn't spread.

## 4. Every feature should create content.

The best features are the ones that give you something to say in a video. "I'm locking in Bijan as my RB1." "Only 9% of users have him that high." "My WR accuracy last year: #21 out of 41,000." If a feature doesn't generate a sentence worth saying on camera, it's a utility, not a product.

## 5. Seasonal events should feel like launches.

The Oracle Challenge isn't a form — it's an event. The countdown, the lock, the results reveal — each phase should feel like something is happening. Draft season has natural energy. Use it.

## 6. Every profile should tell a story, not just display numbers.

"QB Score: 88.2" is a number. "You nailed 7 of your Top 10 QBs — best call: Josh Allen at #1" is a story. Numbers are the raw material. The product's job is to turn them into something a person would actually say out loud.

## 7. The competition creates the reputation.

The tier builder is a tool. The Oracle Challenge is a credential. Someone who finishes #14 out of 42,000 in WR accuracy has earned something they can point to. Features that build toward that — rankings, predictions, scoring, profiles — are core. Features that don't, aren't.

## 8. Don't build for hypothetical users.

Build for the people who are already here: fantasy players who want to prepare better and creators who want to prove they're the best analyst. If a feature needs a user type that doesn't exist yet, it can wait.

## 9. Simple beats complete.

A ranking tool people actually use beats a comprehensive platform nobody finishes. Ship the smallest version that's worth using, then add what the real users actually ask for.

## 10. The ecosystem compounds.

Content attracts users. Tools prepare them. Competition engages them. Reputation keeps them coming back. Every feature should strengthen at least one layer of that loop — ideally more than one.

---

*These principles exist to make decisions easier, not harder. When two directions feel equally reasonable, use these to pick.*
